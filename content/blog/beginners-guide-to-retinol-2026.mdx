Retinol is the most well-researched over-the-counter anti-aging ingredient available. Decades of clinical evidence confirm it increases cell turnover, stimulates collagen synthesis, and reduces the appearance of fine lines, wrinkles, and uneven skin tone. It works.

But start wrong and you will experience the classic retinol reaction: redness, flaking, and irritation that makes your skin look worse before it looks better. Most beginners who "tried retinol and couldn't tolerate it" simply started too fast with too high a concentration.

Here is how to start correctly.

## Why Retinol Causes Irritation (And Why That's Manageable)

Retinol accelerates skin cell turnover — new cells surface faster than your skin is used to, and the skin's barrier temporarily becomes more permeable and reactive. This period is called retinization, and it is a normal part of your skin adjusting to the ingredient.

The irritation is not permanent and not dangerous. It is your skin adapting. The key is managing the adaptation rate so it does not become uncomfortable.

## The Beginner Schedule

**Weeks 1–4:** Use retinol once every 3 nights. Apply to dry, cleansed skin. Use a pea-sized amount. Follow immediately with moisturizer.

**Weeks 5–8:** If tolerating well (minimal redness, no persistent peeling), increase to every other night.

**Month 3 and beyond:** If tolerated well, move to nightly use.

Do not rush this schedule. The results from retinol are cumulative over months, not days. There is no benefit to increasing frequency faster than your skin tolerates.

## The Sandwich Method for Sensitive Skin

If your skin is particularly reactive, use the "sandwich method": apply moisturizer first, wait 5 minutes, apply your retinol, wait another 5 minutes, apply moisturizer again. This buffers the retinol on both sides and significantly reduces irritation without eliminating efficacy.

## What to Avoid When Starting Retinol

**Do not use retinol and exfoliating acids (AHAs, BHAs) on the same night.** This doubles the cell turnover stimulus and dramatically increases irritation. Use your retinol on one night and any acid exfoliant on a separate night.

**Do not use retinol in the morning.** Retinol degrades in sunlight and makes your skin more sun-sensitive. Always use it at night and wear SPF 30+ the following morning.

**Do not skip SPF.** This is not optional when using retinol. The accelerated cell turnover leaves the fresh new cells on your skin surface more vulnerable to UV damage.
